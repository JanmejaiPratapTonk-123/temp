DANCE WEST BENGAL

Chhau

A vigorous martial dance combining acrobatics, elaborate masks, and mythological storytelling from the heart of eastern India.

The Tradition

Chhau is a traditional dance-drama from eastern India, renowned for its martial origins, vigorous movements, and vibrant, oversized masks. Rooted in the folk traditions of Purulia (West Bengal), Seraikella (Jharkhand), and Mayurbhanj (Odisha), it is deeply intertwined with regional folklore and religious epics like the Ramayana and Mahabharata.

The dance is characterized by energetic leaps, complex footwork, and a lack of dialogue—the narrative is driven entirely by physical expression and the rhythmic accompaniment of traditional instruments like the dhol (drum) and shehnai (reed instrument).

Mask Making

A craft passed down through generations in Charida village.

The Story

Originating as a martial art form performed by local warriors (Paikas), Chhau evolved into a stylized dance to celebrate the spring festival of Chaitra Parva.
